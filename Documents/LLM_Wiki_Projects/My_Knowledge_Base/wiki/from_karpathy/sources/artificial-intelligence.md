# Artificial Intelligence

## Summary

Wikipedia article on artificial intelligence (AI), covering its definition as the capability of computational systems to perform tasks associated with human intelligence. The article surveys AI's goals (reasoning, knowledge representation, planning, learning, NLP, perception), techniques (search, logic, neural networks, probabilistic methods), major applications, and ethical risks.

## Key Points

- AI is a field of research in engineering, mathematics, and computer science that develops methods for machines to perceive, learn, reason, and act.
- Traditional goals include reasoning, knowledge representation, planning, natural language processing, perception, and robotics.
- Techniques span state-space search, mathematical optimization, formal logic, artificial neural networks, and statistical methods.
- Deep learning and the transformer architecture (post-2017) have driven the current AI boom, enabling generative AI and large language models.
- High-profile applications include search engines, chatbots, autonomous vehicles, recommendation systems, and strategy games.
- Ethical concerns include privacy, copyright, bias, job displacement, autonomous weapons, and concentration of power among tech giants.

## Evidence / Notes

- AI was founded as an academic discipline in 1956 at the Dartmouth workshop.
- The field has experienced multiple cycles of optimism followed by "AI winters" (periods of reduced funding and interest).
- GPU acceleration of neural networks after 2012 and the transformer architecture after 2017 are key inflection points.
- Generative AI models (GPT, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion) generate text, images, audio, and video from prompts.
- Major AI benchmarks include ImageNet for image classification, the bar exam, SAT, and GRE for LLMs.
