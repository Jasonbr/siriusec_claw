# Generative AI

## Summary

Generative AI is a subfield of artificial intelligence that uses generative models to create new content—text, images, videos, audio, and software code—in response to prompts. The 2020s AI boom was largely driven by advances in generative AI.

## Key Points

- Generative models learn patterns from training data and generate new data that resembles it.
- Large language models (LLMs) like GPT generate text by predicting the next token.
- Text-to-image models include DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, and Adobe Firefly.
- Text-to-video and multimodal generation are rapidly advancing.
- Generative AI is used in software development (vibe coding), healthcare, finance, entertainment, and customer service.
- Concerns include copyright infringement, deepfakes, hallucinations, and misuse for non-consensual content.

## Evidence / Notes

- Source: [artificial-intelligence Wikipedia article](../sources/artificial-intelligence.md)
- The prevalence of generative AI increased significantly with the AI boom of the 2020s.
- Training on unlicensed copyrighted works has raised legal questions about fair use.
- The "AI Hallucination Cases" database was established in 2025 to track legal issues arising from AI-generated misinformation.

## Open Questions

- Can generative AI be made reliably truthful, or are hallucinations an inherent limitation of probabilistic generation?
- How should copyright law adapt to AI systems trained on human-created works?
- What safeguards are needed to prevent generative AI from enabling large-scale disinformation or fraud?
